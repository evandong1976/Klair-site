## Design Specificity Verdict

**Half-specific: the skin and the hero are Klair's; the skeleton is every dev-tool page.**

**LLM assessment.** What no other product could lift:
- The flower is a mechanic, not a logo: bloom is sampling resolution stepping from 11 to 30.
- Reforma 1918 is the only typeface, and keycaps are the recurring component, with buttons that press like keys.
- Feature icons are authored as 12×12 pixel maps.
- The copy uses exact numbers (4 s, 5%, 15k characters, 14 days).

What any product could use unchanged:
- A glass-pill eyebrow over a two-line headline with a coloured second line, a dark and light button pair, and a product shot in a browser frame.
- Six repeats of uppercase label + h2 + paragraph, a 3×2 icon-card grid, numbered steps, an FAQ accordion, and a centred closing CTA.
- Fade-up scroll reveal on about 30 blocks. The app's own spec says "No other decorative motion" beyond the flower's hop and bloom (`Klair/design/DESIGN.md:118`).

Missed product character:
- **The page doesn't do what the product does.** Klair sits in the corner of what you read and offers help when you linger; the page shows that inside a shrunken fake browser instead of doing it to the visitor.
- **The demo shows walls of text.** The spec describes answers as composed UI, a slider driving a live plot (`DESIGN.md:211-213`). All four demo answers are static prose.
- **The paused flower is never shown.** `flower.js` implements the greyscale paused state and nothing on the site uses it, though it is the most reassuring image a screen-recording tool has.

**Deterministic scan.** 31 findings in `index.html` (21 warnings, 10 advisories; the CLI gives no line numbers). 19 are real and 12 are false positives.

**Visual overlays.** The in-page run reported 12 findings on 11 elements: the six icon tiles, two rose badges, one nested card, and skipped-heading and cream-palette in the top banner. One extra hit, `text-occlusion`, was the Claude-in-Chrome extension's own indicator, not the page.

## Overall Impression

The first screen is the best thing here: the bloom, a streamed answer, then highlights landing on the article is a real moment, in a visual language nobody else has. Then the page turns into a README. The single biggest opportunity is to cut the persuade page down to what earns trust and move the manual one click away.

## What's Working

1. **The flower carries identity and status in one object.** Resolution stands for attention, so the bloom means something, and the stepped motion is honest to the pixels.
2. **The demo proves rather than claims.** Provenance chips and marks on the page show "grounded" directly, it works by mouse and keyboard, and it has real exits.
3. **The copy is specific and the guide anticipates failure.** Exact thresholds, a "Check it worked" block with expected output, and Needed/Recommended/Optional tags. That precision is why the overclaims in issue 1 stand out.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The hero's trust claims contradict the page's own privacy section**
- **What:** The hero says "Nothing runs until you ask" (`index.html:57`) and "runs on your Mac" (`:47`). The page also says "Every few seconds Klair checks the screen" (`:158`), "A vision model writes a short note" (`:226`), and lists "Each new screen, to write its note" under Sent to AI models (`:639`). "Screenshots" sits under Kept on your Mac (`:629`), and "Images and files never leave your Mac" (`:649`) is true only of the optional cloud copy.
- **Why it matters:** Trust is the conversion lever for a screen-recording tool. A careful reader finds the contradiction in under a minute, and someone who believed the hero later finds API charges for calls they never "asked" for.
- **Fix:**
  - Replace the two hero claims with accurate ones: stored on your Mac, screenshots go to the model you choose, answers only when you pick.
  - Say "a screenshot of each new screen" in the Sent card.
  - Scope the "never leave" line to the cloud copy.
  - Add a spend-cap tip at the API key step. No cost figure: the last commit dropped an unverified one.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

**[P1] Install is a 5,000px manual with gaps at the high-stakes moments**
- **What:**
  - Install is 43% of the desktop page, with no step ids, no progress, and optional steps 4, 7 and 8 fully expanded.
  - Step 1's single Copy pastes `xcode-select --install`, the Homebrew installer and `brew install` together (`:323-325`). On a fresh Apple-silicon Mac `brew` isn't on PATH until the installer's "next steps" are run, so the third line likely fails. This is from Homebrew's normal behaviour; nobody ran it.
  - `cp .env.example .env.local` (`:379`) overwrites the key file on a re-run.
  - The mock "Allow", "Start session" and "Load unpacked" look clickable and do nothing.
  - Screen Recording (step 6) and Input Monitoring (step 8) ask for the scariest permissions with the privacy answer about 3,000px away.
- **Why it matters:** Install is the conversion. A first-timer who fails at command three has no recovery path on the page.
- **Fix:**
  - Add step ids and a sticky progress rail, and collapse optional steps.
  - Split step 1 into three copies with the PATH line, and use `cp -n`.
  - Strip the button affordance from mocks.
  - Add one reassurance sentence at each permission and a "Stuck here?" link per step.
  - Add a five-line quickstart for people who have the toolchain.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard`

**[P1] The hero demo has visible defects in its showcase state**
- **What:**
  - Badge "1" sits on top of the formula its own note calls "the whole method in one line" (`styles.css:178`).
  - The panel's hint strip is clipped by `overflow: hidden` (`styles.css:211`).
  - "Ask for something else…" looks like an input, but typing "f" or "k" closes the demo (`main.js:395`).
  - Tab lands on two invisible stops, because `.answer { display: flex }` overrides the `hidden` attribute (`styles.css:245`).
  - The tour starts at 35% visibility (`main.js:428`) and ends in about six seconds, so on a 934px-tall window it had finished before it was scrolled to. There is no replay.
- **Why it matters:** This is the page's main persuasion device and the first thing a careful visitor pokes.
- **Fix:**
  - Anchor badges in a left gutter.
  - Fit the panel content or let it scroll.
  - Make the ask box real or plainly inert.
  - Put `inert` on closed states.
  - Add a Replay control and start the tour only when the frame is mostly in view.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] The phone layout fails where it matters**
- **What:** These were measured in a 390px emulation, not on a real device.
  - Nav links are `display: none` at 760px and below with no replacement (`styles.css:461`), on a page about 20,000px long.
  - The demo scales to 0.57, so article text is 8.5px and keycaps 5.4px, and the panel covers most of both highlights.
  - 22 of 42 touch targets are under 44px, including all eight Copy buttons (47×24).
  - Hints say "press K" and "Hover a number" on a touch device.
  - The closing CTA's Copy button is clipped off the right edge.
  - Nobody can install on a phone, and the page offers no hand-off.
- **Why it matters:** Links to student tools travel through group chats, so the first look is often on a phone, where the page can neither demonstrate the product nor send the visitor to their Mac.
- **Fix:**
  - Give phone its own demo composition at real type sizes with tap-to-reveal notes.
  - Add a compact section menu.
  - Reflow the diagram vertically and wrap the closing pill.
  - Make "send this link to my Mac" the phone primary.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

**[P2] One scroll does four jobs, in the category's default order**
- **What:**
  - Landing page, manual, shortcut reference and FAQ share about 12,000px.
  - Privacy starts 81% of the way down, after the page has asked for a clone, an API key and Screen Recording.
  - The pipeline is told twice, as four steps and then as a diagram of the same four.
  - The dark primary button points at GitHub in the nav, install in the hero, and "Star Klair on GitHub" at the close, so the last ask is a favour.
- **Why it matters:** The visitor deciding whether to trust Klair wades through reference material, and reassurance arrives after the ask.
- **Fix:**
  - Cut the persuade page to about five screens.
  - Move Install, Keys and Troubleshooting to a guide page or behind disclosure.
  - Put Privacy before Install.
  - Make the closing button the install.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

## Persona Red Flags

- "Help that stays on your screen." doesn't say what Klair is, the lede is one 45-word sentence, and the fine print drops "OpenRouter key" unexplained.
- Clicks "Set it up on your Mac" expecting a download and gets "built from source" (`:304`).
- Step 1's bundled Copy likely ends in `command not found: brew`, with no troubleshooting entry.
- Reads the ⌘1 keycap on a demo tile literally and switches browser tabs.

**Riley (stress tester):**

- Types in the ask box and the demo closes.
- Clicks the three progress bars under the demo; they're inert.
- Re-runs `cp` and loses the key.
- Turns JavaScript off: about 30 scroll-reveal blocks stay at `opacity: 0` with no fallback (`styles.css:447`; from source, not tested).

**Casey (mobile):**
- No nav, no thumb-zone action, and no way to hand the page off to a Mac.
- The demo tour has finished before they reach it, and its text is 5–8px.
- Link previews carry no image: there is no `og:image` and the Twitter card is `summary`.

## Minor Observations

- Old-style figures make "⌘1" read like "⌘ı" in keycaps and step badges. The font ships lining figures; they're unused.
- The rose focus ring is 2.4:1 on the cream ground, under the 3:1 bar. It's fine on the dark terminals.
- There is no skip link, eight buttons are all named "Copy", and "Copied" isn't announced.
- More than 20 font sizes in half-pixel steps (11, 11.5, 12, 12.5 and so on).
- "Quiz me" prints the answers directly under the questions.
- The hero's background flowers are 12–14-cell flowers stretched to 300–440px at low opacity, so they read as stray pastel blocks, and on phone they sit behind the lede.
- Status squares change meaning: rose is "optional" in step 2 and "sent to AI" in Privacy.

## Questions to Consider

1. Klair's idea is a flower in the corner of what you're reading that offers help when you linger. Why isn't this page doing that to the visitor?
2. Is this a landing page or a README in a nicer typeface? What is lost if it's five screens and the manual is one click away?
3. Who is it for today: a student, or a developer who studies? If a non-developer can't get past step 1, should the hero button say "Set it up" or "Tell me when there's a download"?
